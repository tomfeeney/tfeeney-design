# Portfolio Content Audit — Tom Feeney

## Recommended positioning

Use **Senior Product Designer** as the primary professional identity.

Use **enterprise UX, AI-powered products, data-heavy platforms, interaction design, and design systems** as supporting specialties. UX is the method and competency set; Product Design is the role recruiters should remember.

Recommended positioning line:

> Senior Product Designer for enterprise, AI, and data-intensive products.

Supporting line:

> I turn complex workflows and dense information into clear, scalable products—from discovery and product strategy through interaction design and polished delivery.

## The 10-second portfolio story

1. **Who:** Senior Product Designer with 15+ years of experience.
2. **Where you are strongest:** Enterprise, AI, data, operational products, and complex workflows.
3. **How you work:** Product strategy, research, interaction design, prototyping, and systems thinking.
4. **Proof:** Apple Topline, Audi ADAS HMI, Tesla Fleet & Energy, and Apple Maps QA tooling.

## Recommended project order

1. **Apple Topline** — Lead story: enterprise AI, product strategy, reporting workflows, design systems.
2. **Audi ADAS HMI** — Safety-critical interaction design, human factors, cross-display systems.
3. **Tesla Fleet & Energy** — Operational products, dashboards, real-time workflows, enterprise design system.
4. **Apple Maps Survey Tool** — Internal tooling, workflow redesign, measurable efficiency improvement.
5. **eBay HUB** — Earlier supporting work showing simple, human product design and adoption.
6. **AI Sandbox / Kaijū Run** — Separate experimental work; do not place it on the same level as the core professional case studies.

## Main issues found in the original content

- The site alternated between **UX Designer**, **UI/UX Designer**, and **Product Designer**, weakening the primary role signal.
- Case studies repeated the same vocabulary: “fragmented,” “clarity,” “trust,” “scalable,” “end-to-end,” and “systems.”
- AI tools such as ChatGPT and Dovetail were sometimes more prominent than the product decisions and user outcomes.
- “My Role” appeared near the bottom of most case studies, forcing readers to infer ownership.
- The homepage process and skills sections contained too many steps and AI-specific buzzwords, including capabilities not emphasized by the résumé.
- Kaijū Run included production-level asset and mechanic inventories that distracted from the relevant design story.
- eBay was presented with the same narrative weight as newer, more relevant work.

## Changes made

### Homepage

- Positioned Tom consistently as a **Senior Product Designer**.
- Rewrote the hero and About copy around enterprise, AI, data, product strategy, and systems thinking.
- Reduced the process section to four scannable stages.
- Replaced the long AI-jargon skill inventory with four focused capability groups.
- Moved testimonials directly after selected work to establish credibility earlier.
- Clarified that AI Sandbox projects support, rather than replace, the professional case studies.
- Updated metadata, canonical URLs, Open Graph URLs, and schema references to `tfeeney-design.com`.

### Case studies

- Moved **Role & Scope** directly below the overview on every core case study.
- Replaced repeated generic language with project-specific decisions and constraints.
- Reduced emphasis on the tools used for synthesis; increased emphasis on ownership, product decisions, collaboration, and outcomes.
- Aligned role language with the résumé:
  - Apple Topline: Senior Product Designer
  - Audi: Senior Product Designer
  - Tesla: Senior UI/UX Designer, Enterprise Team
  - Apple Maps: Senior UI/UX Designer
- Shortened Kaijū Run by removing detailed asset, character, and mechanic inventories.
- Shortened eBay and positioned it as an earlier supporting case study.

## Metrics and claims to verify before publishing

Keep strong metrics only when you can explain their source during an interview:

- Apple Maps: **60% reduction in survey creation time**.
- eBay: **hundreds of messages in the first month**.
- Any claim about the exact number of Tesla teams using the Enterprise Style Guide.
- Any statement implying a feature shipped, launched, or produced a measured result if the work ended at concept or handoff.

If a number cannot be defended, use a directional outcome instead: “reduced setup steps,” “improved consistency,” “rolled out across multiple teams,” or “made report logic easier to inspect.”

## Recommended case-study structure

Each professional case study should answer these questions in this order:

1. What product problem existed?
2. What was Tom’s role and scope?
3. What did research reveal?
4. What product decisions did Tom make?
5. What was designed or shipped?
6. What changed as a result?

Target approximately **600–850 words of core narrative**, with visuals carrying the rest of the story.
